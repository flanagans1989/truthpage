Not legal advice — a plain-language starting point. Your DPA with each customer may set a stricter notice period or a different channel than the article itself requires; theirs governs.

What the article actually says

GDPR Article 28(2) lets a processor use sub-processors only with the controller's prior written authorization — general or specific — and, where that authorization is general, requires the processor to inform the controller of any intended changes concerning the addition or replacement of sub-processors, giving the controller an opportunity to object.

Three things fall out of that wording that are easy to miss:

  • It's triggered by intent — the notice is supposed to go out before the new sub-processor starts processing data, not as an after-the-fact FYI.
  • It only covers additions and replacements. A sub-processor's own sub-processors, or a change to what an existing one already does, isn't in scope unless your DPA says otherwise.
  • The article doesn't set a notice period. Most DPAs do (30 days is common) — that number comes from your contract, not from GDPR itself.

A template

Adjust the bracketed parts and the notice period to match your own DPA.

Subject: Notice of new sub-processor — [Your Company] Dear [Customer], In accordance with Article 28(2) of the GDPR and our Data Processing Agreement, we are writing to notify you of an intended change to our sub-processors used in providing [Service Name]. Sub-processor: [Vendor name] Purpose: [What it does — e.g. "email delivery", "cloud hosting"] Location of processing: [Country/region] Effective date: [Date, no earlier than [notice period] from this notice] If you wish to object to this sub-processor, please contact us at [contact email] within [notice period] of this notice, stating your grounds. We will work with you in good faith to address a reasonable objection. [Your Company]
