Why teams pay for this

The objection window is short and it starts without you

Under GDPR Article 28(2), a processor working from a general authorisation has to tell you before it adds or replaces a sub-processor, and you get a window to object — typically 10 to 30 days in a commercial DPA. In practice that notice is a quiet update to a page on their site. If nobody is watching the page, the window closes on its own.

"When did that change?" is a question you have to answer with a date

A screenshot taken the week of the audit proves nothing about what the page said three months ago. Every change here is stored with its diff and its timestamp, from the day you started monitoring.

Your customers ask you the same question you ask your vendors

The trust page turns your answer into something you publish once instead of writing out in every security questionnaire — a public, dated history of your sub-processor changes that customers can subscribe to.

Questions

Does this help with DORA?

Partly, and it's worth being precise about which part. DORA requires EU financial entities to keep a Register of Information covering their ICT providers and those providers' subcontractors, filed with the regulator each year. TrustPages keeps the subcontractor half of that current and dated. It is not a filing tool and it does not produce the register for you.

How does the AI tell material changes from cosmetic ones?

Every detected diff is classified as material or cosmetic. Cosmetic changes — typos, reformatting, a moved heading — publish automatically. Anything that could affect your customers, such as a new sub-processor or a new processing country, goes to your approval queue first. You always have the final say before it reaches your trust page.
